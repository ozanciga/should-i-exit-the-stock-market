How this tool works — what it calculates and why
▼

The core problem: Selling a taxable investment triggers a capital gains tax bill immediately. That tax reduces the cash available to redeploy. Even if you're right about a crash coming, the question is: is the crash large enough to offset the tax you paid to exit?

What this calculates: It compares two paths — (A) you exit now, pay tax, hold cash, then re-enter at some point during or after the crash; vs. (B) you hold through the crash and recover. At the point both scenarios return to today's price, path A only wins if the crash was deep enough and re-entry was early enough.

Re-entry timing matters enormously. Re-entering late (market mostly recovered) means you paid tax and gained little. Re-entering at the exact bottom maximises the benefit. The tool models five re-entry points: R0 (perfect bottom) through R4 (back at full price).

RRSP — an important distinction: If you sell investments and convert to cash within your RRSP (staying registered), no tax is triggered. The tax only comes when you actually withdraw money from the plan. This means an RRSP investor can move to cash and re-enter freely with zero tax cost — mathematically equivalent to a TFSA for timing purposes. Only a full RRSP withdrawal triggers income tax on the entire amount.

Reading the results table: Each cell shows two numbers. The dollar figure is the raw net gain or loss. The percentage below it — net benefit ÷ current portfolio value — is the scale-independent version of the same result: it tells you how much larger or smaller your portfolio would be at full recovery compared to simply holding. A +8% means you end up 8% ahead regardless of whether your portfolio is $50k or $5M. This is the more useful number for judging whether the edge is meaningful — a best-case of +0.5% barely justifies the stress and timing risk; a best-case of +20% is a different conversation.

Purpose of this tool: This tool does not predict crashes or advise you to exit. It answers a precise mathematical question: assuming a crash of size X occurs, and assuming you can time exit and re-entry with a given accuracy — under what circumstances does exiting make financial sense?
